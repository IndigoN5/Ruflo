## You already run AI. Who's governing the thinking behind it?

Most city governments have already deployed AI — resident-facing chatbots, case-management triage, permitting workflows, internal drafting tools. The exposure already exists. The question isn't whether to adopt AI; it's whether anything is governing the reasoning these systems produce before that reasoning becomes a decision, a denial, a public-facing answer, or a record.

That's the gap NuvaBase closes. Our methodology, **TLRW**, governs an AI system's thinking *before* it acts — not just its output after the fact:

- **Think** — identify the real problem the system is being asked to solve
- **Learn** — synthesize only the highest-value, verifiable information; separate fact from assumption
- **Reflect (×3)** — triple-check logic, grounding, and output-risk before anything is produced
- **Write** — compressed, precise, immediately actionable output

TLRW is model-agnostic. It doesn't replace the AI systems a city already runs — it governs them, regardless of vendor.

Stage 1 has a start and an end. It is not a retainer, and it does not require the city to commit beyond the audit itself.

## Why Now

The risk isn't hypothetical and it isn't future-tense. It's running in production today, in systems your office already approved. Waiting for an incident — a wrongful denial, a biased recommendation, a public records request that surfaces unreviewed AI reasoning — is the expensive way to discover the gap. An audit is the cheap way.

## Why TLRW

Most AI governance products police outputs after the model has already decided. TLRW governs the decision *process* itself — the thinking layer that produces the output — which means it catches what output-only review structurally cannot: bad reasoning that happens to produce a plausible-looking answer. It is not tied to whichever vendor or model the city currently uses, so adopting it does not require replacing existing systems.

## Who This Is For

This offer is structured for the **city manager's office** — the office that owns operational and legal accountability for how city systems make decisions. Council members and IT/innovation staff are useful entry points into a conversation, but the audit and retainer are scoped as a city manager's office decision.
