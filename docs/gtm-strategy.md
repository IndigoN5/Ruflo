# GTM & Governance Consulting Strategy

Working strategy document, written as a business development / GTM lead would frame it. Source: founder intake interview (two rounds of qualifying questions). Several items below are marked **OPEN** — they are real gaps, not guesses dressed up as facts. Close them before sequencing locks in.

## The picture, as it stands

Three initiatives exist in parallel. They are mostly independent right now, by design:

| Initiative | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **TLRW** (Think / Learn / Reflect / Write) | A client-facing product/methodology — not an internal habit, something meant to be delivered to clients | Named, not running |
| **Nuva** | The "base" TLRW is meant to run on, serving as a governance process | Undefined — no spec, no account, no build confirmed |
| **Governance Process Consulting** | Advisory service sold to "Blue State" city governments (Democrat-run), scope currently east of the Mississippi — e.g. South Fulton GA, Atlanta, Chicago, NYC | Not yet packaged or sold |
| **GHL (Go High Level)** | CRM + sales funnel + outreach/lead-gen engine for selling the consulting service | Account exists, named, unconfigured for ~9 months |

Confirmed relationship: **GHL sells. TLRW/Nuva is the thing being built, separately.** GHL is not specific to TLRW — it's general business infrastructure that should be running regardless of how TLRW/Nuva resolves.

## OPEN: What Nuva actually is

This is the single biggest blocker. Two rounds of clarifying questions came back "no preference," which most likely means it hasn't been decided yet rather than that it doesn't matter. Right now "Nuva" could be:

- An existing platform/account already paid for or signed up for
- A name chosen for something not yet built
- A partner organization

These three branches require completely different next actions (configure vs. spec-and-build vs. negotiate-a-relationship). **Don't sequence Initiative 1 further until this is resolved** — everything else (consulting offer, GHL messaging) can move in parallel without an answer here.

**Recommended unblock step:** a 15-minute self-interview, written down, answering only: *"If Nuva already existed today, what would I be logging into? A URL? A piece of paper? Someone else's product?"* If the honest answer is "nothing exists," that's the answer — Nuva is currently a name for a not-yet-spec'd governance framework, and the next action is writing a one-page spec, not shopping for software.

## Initiative 1 — TLRW / Nuva (client-facing product)

Since TLRW is explicitly client-facing (not personal practice), it needs a sellable shape before it's worth investing further build time:

1. Write a one-page TLRW offer brief: what a client receives, in what format (workshop? recurring engagement? software seat?), and what "governance process" means operationally for a city client.
2. Resolve Nuva (see above) — it's the infrastructure/IP layer under the offer brief, not a separate product to market on its own yet.
3. Pilot with one real or simulated city scenario (even hypothetical South Fulton) to pressure-test the offer brief before it's GTM-ready.

This initiative is currently the least concrete of the three and should not block the other two.

## Initiative 2 — Governance Process Consulting (the offer being sold)

Target market, as defined: city governments under Democratic administration, scoped to east of the Mississippi for now. Named examples: South Fulton GA, Atlanta, Chicago, New York.

Note for accuracy: South Fulton is its own incorporated city in Fulton County, Georgia (not a neighborhood/district "of Atlanta") — worth using the correct framing in outreach material since municipal buyers will notice.

Next actions:
1. Define the offer independent of TLRW for now (governance process consulting can be sold as advisory/facilitation even before TLRW/Nuva is fully built — the methodology can be delivered manually first, productized later).
2. Build a target list: city governments (Democrat-led) east of the Mississippi, prioritized by population size and known openness to outside governance consulting (look at recent procurement activity, council agendas, RFPs).
3. Identify the actual buyer inside a city government (city manager's office, council staff, a specific department) — "the city" is not a buyer, a person/role is.

## Initiative 3 — GHL (sales engine)

Unconfigured for ~9 months; confirmed scope: **CRM + sales funnel + outreach/lead-gen**, all four, not staged.

Concrete build order (this is infrastructure work, not strategy — sequence it so each step is usable on its own):
1. **Account hygiene** — confirm sub-account/snapshot setup, business profile, domain/email sending identity (this is almost certainly still default since "only the name was put on it").
2. **CRM pipeline** — one pipeline modeling the actual sales motion for governance consulting (e.g. stages: Identified → Contacted → Discovery Call → Proposal → Pilot → Client).
3. **Outreach/lead-gen** — sequences targeting the Initiative 2 target list once it exists; don't build outreach automation before the list and buyer persona exist, or it'll automate the wrong message at scale.
4. **Funnel** — a landing page + booking flow for inbound (e.g. a city official who heard about the consulting and wants a call), distinct from the outbound sequences in step 3.

GHL work can start **today**, independent of Nuva being resolved — it's selling a service offer (Initiative 2), and that offer can exist in advisory form before TLRW/Nuva is product-ready.

## "Network" — left open, working interpretation

No preference was given on what "build my network" means (lead pipeline vs. partners/advisors vs. personal tracking system). Rather than guess one and discard the others, the GHL CRM pipeline (Initiative 3, step 2) can be built to hold all three as different contact types/tags from day one — leads, partners/advisors, and personal contacts — so the question doesn't need to be re-decided later, it's just three pipelines instead of one.

## Where AI agents fit (the original ask)

The founder's original framing was "AI agents to think through the processes" — here's where that's actually leverage, once each initiative has enough shape to automate against:

- **Recurring intake agent** — re-runs a short structured interview on a schedule (e.g. weekly) to chip away at the Nuva OPEN item and other undefined pieces, rather than relying on one big conversation to resolve everything at once.
- **Target-list research agent** — given Initiative 2's buyer criteria, researches and scores candidate cities (council activity, budget cycles, prior consulting spend) into a ranked list for GHL outreach.
- **Outreach drafting agent** — drafts first-touch sequences per persona for GHL once the target list and offer brief exist; human reviews before sending.
- **Offer-brief co-writer** — used once, interactively, to turn the TLRW one-page brief (Initiative 1, step 1) from a voice-memo-style description into a sellable document.

None of these are built yet — this section is a backlog, not a delivered feature. Build order should follow the initiatives above: GHL pipeline + target list first (fastest path to revenue motion), Nuva definition second (unblocks Initiative 1), agents layered in once there's a real process to automate rather than a blank page.

## Immediate next actions, in order

1. Resolve what Nuva is (self-interview above) — 15 minutes, unblocks Initiative 1.
2. Write the one-page Governance Process Consulting offer brief (Initiative 2) — sellable today, doesn't need Nuva.
3. Log into GHL and do account hygiene (Initiative 3, step 1) — pure unblocking work, no strategy needed.
4. Build the target list (Initiative 2, step 2) using the buyer criteria above.
5. Stand up the GHL CRM pipeline with three contact types (leads / partners / personal) per the "network" note above.

Steps 1–3 can happen in parallel; they don't depend on each other.
